How the math works

Full Kelly fraction: f = (b·p − (1−p)) / b, where b is the profit per 1 risked and p is the chance you win. For a contract at price c with no fee, this works out to (p − c) / (1 − c).

Trust in your model blends your chance with the market's: used = trust·yours + (1−trust)·market. At 50%, a model that says 56% against a 50% market gets sized as 53%. This is the main protection against a model that's overconfident.

Kalshi fee: the taker fee is 0.07 × P × (1−P) per contract and the maker fee is 0.0175 × P × (1−P). The fee is added to what each contract costs, so it shrinks the payout. Kalshi rounds fees up to the cent per order and some markets use a multiplier, so small orders cost a little more than shown.

Size together: when bets settle at the same time you can't reinvest one win before the next bet, so adding up each bet's own Kelly stake overbets. With this box ticked, the page finds the stakes that maximize expected log bankroll over every combination of wins and losses (up to 12 bets), then applies your fraction and caps. It assumes the bets are independent. Two sides of the same game, or a bet and a parlay that contains it, are correlated, so size them as one bet.

Why fractional: betting a fraction c of Kelly keeps about c·(2−c) of full Kelly's growth with only c² of its variance. At ½ Kelly that's 75% of the growth with 25% of the variance. At 2× Kelly growth drops to zero.

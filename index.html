How to read it: each row is a team’s remaining season, one cell per leg, numbers are its win probability there (horizon-widened — a December cell is a forecast of a line, not a line). Future value adds up how much better than a coin flip those spots are (only the part above 60% counts, because a 55% spot is not a survivor pick) — it is the same number the field model and the champion model use, and the teams at the top are the ones you want to still be holding in December. Cost to burn is what taking the team this week knocks off your best remaining path — the board’s “Future cost” column, shown here beside the schedule it comes from. The brightest cell in a row is the best spot the team has left. Holiday spots matter out of proportion: W12a, W16a are a separate contest week with a short team list, and running out of eligible teams for one is the most common way a live entry actually dies. Free for = how many of your entries have not spent the team yet.

Look Ahead

A look ahead is a forecast of a forecast. Nothing on this tab is a pick, and the lines it prices do not exist yet — a future leg's number is this week's market rating widened for everything that can still happen between now and then (injuries, form, the line itself moving). Use it to see which weeks are thin, what a pick this week costs you later, and which teams to keep — not to commit. The week you are actually submitting is on This week's suggestions.
Every future leg is priced from the same market-implied ratings and Elo the rest of the site uses, widened by 0.32 points of spread per week of lookahead and tilted by each team's expected trajectory. The field is rolled forward through the fitted Circa choice model from Circa W1 selections PDF (graded): 16,968 entries alive now, spending teams every week — 120 simulated seasons of 6,000 entries, each season sharing one outcome per game so entries on the same team live and die together. Survivor counts are projections with a few percent of simulation error around them and are shown to two figures for that reason. Your assumptions live in this browser only.

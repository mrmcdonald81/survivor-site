How to read it: each row is a team’s remaining season, one cell per leg, numbers are its win probability there (horizon-widened — a December cell is a forecast of a line, not a line). Future value adds up how much better than a coin flip those spots are (only the part above 60% counts, because a 55% spot is not a survivor pick) — it is the same number the field model and the champion model use, and the teams at the top are the ones you want to still be holding in December. Cost to burn is what taking the team this week knocks off your best remaining path — the board’s “Future cost” column, shown here beside the schedule it comes from. The brightest cell in a row is the best spot the team has left. Holiday spots matter out of proportion: none left are a separate contest week with a short team list, and running out of eligible teams for one is the most common way a live entry actually dies. Free for = how many of your entries have not spent the team yet.

How past Circa champions played it

Leg-by-leg analysis of the 480 champion picks: average pick 75% to win; the #1 favourite only 31% of the time (top-2 48%, top-3 59%); their pick carried 27% of the field on average while the chalk carried 41%; only 5% of picks were under 60% to win. Chalk rate by season phase: 38% (legs 1–6), 40% (7–12), 17% (13+). Holiday legs: average 64% to win. Champions who owned several entries split them in 83% of legs where two or more were alive — and the extra entries went a rung or two down: average 72% to win (vs 80% for the primary), median #4 favourite, 38% of the time 10+ points below the primary, and a third of them on bottom-half future-value teams (e.g. 2025 Week 1: DEN on the main entry, ARI / JAX / PIT on the others).

What a champion would do this week

A choice model fitted on all 480 champion picks (with each champion's remaining teams and the field's actual pick share that leg) says a champion in your seat picks: LA 65%, SF 11%, NE 10%, BUF 4%, BAL 3%. Their extra entries were played differently — a second model fitted on the 360 secondary picks of champion owners (avg 72% to win, median #4 favourite, a third of them low-future-value teams) gives entry 2: SF 31%, NE 29%, BUF 13%, BAL 9%. With 3 entries that becomes LA / SF / NE. What the fit learned: champions treat a 75% and a 90% favourite almost alike, avoid burning teams with future value, prefer home teams, do not shy from Thursday games, and lean with the field when its share is high — their edge came from the paths they kept, not from fading chalk for its own sake.
